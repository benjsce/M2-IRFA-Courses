## Énoncé
Show that if $\nabla^2f(x)=L\,I$, then minimizing the quadratic approximation of $f$ at $x$ leads to gradient descent. With what step size?

## Solution officielle
None on the slides.

## Résolution
The second-order approximation at $x$ is $m(h)=f(x)+\nabla f(x)^\top h+\tfrac12h^\top\nabla^2f(x)h$. With $\nabla^2f(x)=LI$ it becomes $m(h)=f(x)+\nabla f(x)^\top h+\tfrac L2\|h\|^2$, a strongly convex quadratic in $h$. Its gradient $\nabla f(x)+Lh$ vanishes at $h=-\tfrac1L\nabla f(x)$, so the next point is $x-\tfrac1L\nabla f(x)$: a gradient step, with step size $1/L$.

When $f$ is only $L$-smooth, the Hessian is not $LI$, but the same model with $L$ is an upper bound of $f$ (Proposition 2.4), so the step guarantees $f(x-\tfrac1L\nabla f(x))\leq f(x)-\tfrac1{2L}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$. On $f(x)=x^2-\cos x$ with $L=3$, from $x=1$: the step lands at $0.053$ and $f$ drops from $0.46$ to $-0.996$.

## Ce que l'exercice a révélé
- ods/gradient-descent: the step $1/L$ is not a tuning choice but the minimizer of the quadratic upper bound; any smaller step also decreases $f$.
