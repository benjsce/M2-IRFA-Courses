## Énoncé
Let $f$ be a differentiable function on an open set $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^p$, and $C\subseteq\Omega$ a convex subset of $\Omega$. Prove that $f$ is strictly convex on $C$ if, and only if, $\forall x,\bar{x}\in C$, $x\neq\bar{x}\implies f(x)>f(\bar{x})+\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),x-\bar{x}\rangle$.

## Solution officielle
None in the notes (the exercise is announced by footnote 1 of §2.2.3).

## Résolution
Strict convexity means $f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)<\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)$ for $x\neq y$ and $\lambda\in(0,1)$.

**If.** Take $x\neq y$ in $C$, $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $z=\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y$. Then $z\neq x$ and $z\neq y$, so the strict tangent inequality applies twice at $\bar{x}=z$: $f(x)>f(z)+\langle\nabla f(z),x-z\rangle$ and $f(y)>f(z)+\langle\nabla f(z),y-z\rangle$. Combining with weights $\alpha$ and $1-\alpha$, the gradient terms cancel because $\alpha(x-z)+(1-\alpha)(y-z)=0$, and $\alpha f(x)+(1-\alpha)f(y)>f(z)$.

**Only if.** The limit argument of Proposition 2.2 only gives a large inequality: strictness is lost when $\lambda\to0$. The fix is to go through the midpoint. Take $x\neq\bar{x}$ and $m=\tfrac12(x+\bar{x})\neq\bar{x}$. Strict convexity gives $f(m)<\tfrac12f(x)+\tfrac12f(\bar{x})$. A strictly convex function is convex, so the large tangent inequality holds at $\bar{x}$: $f(m)\geq f(\bar{x})+\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),m-\bar{x}\rangle=f(\bar{x})+\tfrac12\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),x-\bar{x}\rangle$. Chaining the two and multiplying by $2$: $f(x)>f(\bar{x})+\langle\nabla f(\bar{x}),x-\bar{x}\rangle$.

## Ce que l'exercice a révélé
- ods/first-order-characterization: the passage to the limit that proves the tangent inequality cannot keep a strict inequality; strictness has to be obtained at a finite distance, here the midpoint.
