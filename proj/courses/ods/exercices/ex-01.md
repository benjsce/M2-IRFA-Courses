## Énoncé
Prove that $C$ is convex if, and only if, $\forall(x,y)\in C^2,\ \forall\lambda\in(0,1),\ \lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\in C$.

## Solution officielle
None in the notes.

## Résolution
**Only if.** If $C$ is convex, the segment condition holds for every $\lambda\in[0,1]$, hence in particular for every $\lambda$ in the smaller interval $(0,1)$.

**If.** Assume the condition for $\lambda\in(0,1)$, and take $x,y\in C$. The two missing values are the endpoints: $\lambda=1$ gives $1\cdot x+0\cdot y=x$ and $\lambda=0$ gives $y$, and both are in $C$ by assumption. So the condition holds for every $\lambda\in[0,1]$, which is Definition 2.1.

## Ce que l'exercice a révélé
- ods/convex-set: the endpoints of the segment carry no information, so the open interval $(0,1)$ can be used whenever it is more convenient — for instance in strict inequalities.
