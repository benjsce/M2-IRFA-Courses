## Énoncé
$X$ is sparse with $n=10^5$, $p=10^6$ and 10 non-zeros per row, i.e. $\mathrm{nnz}(X)=10^6$. In float64, how much memory do you need for $X$, for $X^\top X$ and for $XX^\top$?

## Solution officielle
$X$ sparse (CSR): nnz values plus indices, about 12 MB. $X$ dense: $n\times p$, 800 GB. $X^\top X$ (primal): $p\times p$, 8 TB. $XX^\top$ (dual): $n\times n$, 80 GB. The primal normal equations cannot even be stored, let alone solved; $X^\top X$ is dense in general even when $X$ is sparse; the dual is better but still out of reach, and the SVD is not an option either. $X$ itself fits in 12 MB: the problem is not the data, it is the matrices we build from it.

## Résolution
A float64 takes 8 bytes and an int32 index 4.
- CSR: $10^6$ values ($8\times10^6$ bytes), $10^6$ column indices ($4\times10^6$), $n+1$ row pointers ($\approx4\times10^5$): $\approx12.4$ MB.
- Dense $X$: $10^5\times10^6\times8=8\times10^{11}$ bytes, 800 GB.
- $X^\top X$: $(10^6)^2\times8=8\times10^{12}$ bytes, 8 TB.
- $XX^\top$: $(10^5)^2\times8=8\times10^{10}$ bytes, 80 GB.

Why $X^\top X$ is dense: its entry $(j,k)$ is nonzero as soon as some row has non-zeros in both columns $j$ and $k$; with ten non-zeros per row, each row alone fills a $10\times10$ block, and $10^5$ rows scatter such blocks over the matrix.

## Ce que l'exercice a révélé
- ods/sparse-matrix: sparsity does not survive the products that the normal equations build.
- ods/dual-ridge: its memory table is this computation.
