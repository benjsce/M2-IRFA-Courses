## Énoncé
For the quadratic form $q(x)=\tfrac12x^\top Ax-b^\top x+c$, with $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$, $A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$, $b\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and $c\in\mathbb{R}$:
- what equation do stationary points satisfy?
- what condition on $A$ do we need to guarantee the existence and uniqueness of $x^\star$?
- show that minimizing $q$ boils down to solving a linear system.

## Solution officielle
None on the slides.

## Résolution
**Stationary points.** Expanding $q(x+h)$ gives $q(x)+\tfrac12(A+A^\top)x\cdot h-b^\top h+\tfrac12h^\top Ah$, so $\nabla q(x)=\tfrac12(A+A^\top)x-b$. Only the symmetric part of $A$ matters, and one may take $A$ symmetric; then $\nabla q(x)=Ax-b$ and the stationary points solve $Ax=b$. The Hessian is $A$ everywhere.

**Existence and uniqueness.** If $A\succ0$ (symmetric positive definite), $q$ is $\mu$-strongly convex with $\mu=\lambda_{\min}(A)>0$: it has a unique minimizer, and by the first-order condition it is the unique stationary point, $x^\star=A^{-1}b$. If $A$ has a negative eigenvalue with eigenvector $v$, then $q(tv)=\tfrac12\lambda t^2\|v\|^2-t\,b^\top v+c\to-\infty$: no minimizer. If $A\succeq0$ is singular, a minimizer exists if and only if $b$ lies in the range of $A$, and then the minimizers form an affine set, never a single point.

**Linear system.** With $A\succ0$, $q$ is convex, so a point minimizes it exactly when its gradient vanishes, i.e. when $Ax=b$. On the four observations, $A=\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$, $b=(1,2)$: $x^\star=(\tfrac1{11},\tfrac7{11})$ and $q(x^\star)=c-\tfrac12b^\top x^\star$.

## Ce que l'exercice a révélé
- ods/quadratic-form: the symmetry of $A$ is implicit on the slide; without it, the gradient involves $\tfrac12(A+A^\top)$.
- ods/strongly-convex-function: positive definiteness is exactly strong convexity for a quadratic, with the smallest eigenvalue as modulus.

None; the slide does not say that $A$ is symmetric, which the answer assumes.
