## Énoncé
The ridge solution is $\hat{w}=(X^\top X+\lambda I_p)^{-1}X^\top y$. Using the matrix inversion lemma, show that $\hat{w}=X^\top(XX^\top+\lambda I_n)^{-1}y$.
- Using the SVD of $X$, propose an implementation.
- Can you use the SVD to confirm the primal-dual link?
- What if $X$ is sparse, $n$ is $10^5$ and $p$ is $10^6$?

## Solution officielle
None on the slides; slide 12 answers the last question.

## Résolution
**Woodbury.** With $A=\lambda I_p$, $U=X^\top$, $C=I_n$, $V=X$: $(\lambda I_p+X^\top X)^{-1}=\lambda^{-1}I_p-\lambda^{-2}X^\top(I_n+\lambda^{-1}XX^\top)^{-1}X$. Multiply by $X^\top y$ and factor $\lambda^{-1}X^\top$ on the left: $\hat{w}=\lambda^{-1}X^\top\big[I_n-(\lambda I_n+XX^\top)^{-1}XX^\top\big]y$. Writing $I_n=(\lambda I_n+XX^\top)^{-1}(\lambda I_n+XX^\top)$, the bracket is $(\lambda I_n+XX^\top)^{-1}\lambda I_n$, and $\hat{w}=X^\top(XX^\top+\lambda I_n)^{-1}y$. A shorter route: $X^\top(XX^\top+\lambda I_n)=(X^\top X+\lambda I_p)X^\top$, then invert on both sides.

**SVD implementation.** With the thin SVD $X=U\Sigma V^\top$, singular values $\sigma_i$: $X^\top X+\lambda I=V(\Sigma^2+\lambda I)V^\top$ on the span of $V$, so $\hat{w}=V\,\mathrm{diag}\big(\tfrac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_i^2+\lambda}\big)U^\top y$. Computed once, the SVD gives the solution for every $\lambda$ at the cost of a few matrix-vector products.

**Primal-dual link.** In the dual form, $XX^\top+\lambda I_n=U(\Sigma^2+\lambda I)U^\top$ and $X^\top=V\Sigma U^\top$: $X^\top(XX^\top+\lambda I_n)^{-1}y=V\,\mathrm{diag}\big(\tfrac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_i^2+\lambda}\big)U^\top y$, the same expression. On the four observations both forms give $(\tfrac1{11},\tfrac7{11})$ (the primal system is $2\times2$, the dual $4\times4$).

**Sparse, $n=10^5$, $p=10^6$.** The dual matrix is $10^5\times10^5$, 80 GB in float64; the primal $10^6\times10^6$, 8 TB; the SVD is out of reach. Neither closed form can be used: one needs a solver that only computes products $Xw$ and $X^\top v$.

## Ce que l'exercice a révélé
- ods/dual-ridge: the choice between primal and dual is the choice of the smaller of $n$ and $p$; at this scale neither is small.
- ods/singular-value-decomposition: both forms are the same shrinkage of each singular direction, which is the cleanest proof of the link.
